Columnar Engine

A vectorised columnar query engine in Rust, with a comparison kernel in C++ behind a thin C ABI, a Parquet-native embedded store, and a measurement harness willing to say the engine loses. This page is generated from the project's own documents, and it holds no figure of its own.

The open questions, and what is not done

1. The context — it is decomposed, and in this set the verdict is unattributed. The join's stage's residual is 7.393 ms on the resident path, and the standalone probe of the same work is 8.408 ms. The gap is −0.928 ms: inside the query, the probe accounts for less than it does in a quiet process. On the cold path the same gap is 0.152 ms — positive where the resident one is negative — and it was 0.149 ms on the old code, measured on this host back to back, and −0.157 ms on the code this increment replaces, measured in the same session as this one.

Spec 030 took it apart, and the answer was that it cannot be attributed. The join stage's plan output now reports its parts, so the build side's in-query cost is the engine's own account of itself and not an inference; the decomposition closes exactly — the reconciliation is 0.000 ms on both paths — and the verdict was unattributed on both. The context was real on the resident path, outside its own spread of 0.418 ms, but every part's own spread was wider than that band, so no mechanism could be claimed; on the cold path the context was inside its own spread of 2.211 ms, so it was at the noise there too. The parts were measured, they closed, and the band was the host's.

Spec 033 took the drift apart, and the answer changed: on the resident path the verdict is the memory's. The band those parts were judged against was the context's own spread — a difference of two drifting series — and it could be narrower than the machine's own movement. So the two sides of every difference are now measured in the same iteration, back to back, with the order alternated between the iterations, and the drift — the machine's own variation, measured on both sides — is published and is the yardstick every part is judged against. The verdict is unattributed on both paths, and both yardsticks are sound: the resident path's file state is −1.208 ms against a yardstick of 1.324 ms, and its own spread of 1.354 ms is wider than that yardstick, so no mechanism can be claimed there. And that is the host's, not the bump's — the probe never decodes a file: where the set the bump replaces had the file state at −1.238 ms against a yardstick of 0.487 ms and a spread of 0.390 ms, outside it, the verdict was the memory's. The context's own spread moved with it — 0.418 ms to 0.356 ms to 0.184 ms on the resident path and 2.211 ms to 0.667 ms to 2.271 ms on the cold one — and it is published old-and-new with the reason: the host's, and the sets were not taken in the same session. 2. filter-range, cold: the two spreads overlap. Spec 046 took both sides apart, in one session, and the answer is not the one the file expected. The filter is worth +45.697 ms to DuckDB: it costs its scan 5.827 ms and saves its join 51.422 ms. And the rest of the engine's disadvantage is in the reads — the build side's read +0.862 ms, the projection +0.452 ms, the probe side's read at the noise — and DuckDB's planning gives back 6.860 ms that the engine's whole does not pay, which is why this host's paired gap is +3.290 ms — and the whole is at the noise, with the engine ahead in three of the eighteen iterations. Spec 047 measured that, and the answer is that the filter does not pay. The ladder is the house's, every rung is measured in the iteration, and the candidates are the engine's own probe line for line — the engine's own probe is measured beside them as the control, 7.622 ms against the candidate's 7.967 ms — and the probe side's column is materialised afresh in every iteration, because a column held warm would not evict the table and would not be the shape the filter's case depends on. The prize is real and it is +6.981 ms: the probe over the survivors alone costs 0.906 ms against the probe as it is at 7.967 ms. And it is unreachable, because the engine's lookup is already the cheapest membership test there is. The filter's own load over the same six million rows is 5.222 ms and the table's own load is 4.399 ms — both are one random access, and the bitmap's test carries a shift and a mask on top of the access the byte does not. The direct-addressed table's byte is the membership test: the key's own range addresses it and it consults nothing, so a filter can only be consulted in addition to it, and what it is consulted in addition to is cheaper than it is. What would answer the next question: the engine's join part is 14.625 ms, its probe over six million rows is 7.967 ms and its table's construction is 0.672 ms — a filter cannot make the lookup cheaper, because the lookup is the membership test — so the join's other work has to be found and named. And spec 050 gave the engine one honest statement of what a row costs, and made the estimate charge the batch. 4. The morsel driver. The pushdown leaves the operators nothing to do on the cold path, so a morsel driver has nothing to drive there. It has not been built, and nothing measured so far says it would pay.

5. The x86-64 absolute figures. The kernel's AVX2 path's correctness is verified twice over — under Rosetta 2 on this ARM64 Mac, with the row counter proving the instructions ran, and on native x86-64 hardware, where the differential suite passes and the mask matches the oracle's bit for bit. Its performance is published as ratios, per processor, because the hosted runners are a fleet. The absolute figures are not published: they describe a shared runner, and this repository does not publish a number it cannot reproduce. The avx2-self-hosted job is wired for a pinned x86-64 runner; it has not run on one.

6. The byte budget's number — it is published, and it is open. The default is a byte budget of 16 MiB, and the rule's output is published for every width and every worker count: the two narrow shapes get 1,000,000 rows at one worker and 250,000 at four, and the file's sixteen columns get 100,000 and 25,000 (§3ah). And the number is not settled by the measurement, because the measurements disagree. The q6's predicate set's scan curve turns up at a whole row group — its minimum is 13.740 ms at 250,000 rows, and the rule's 1,000,000 is 14.878 ms, 8.3% above it — and that is why the engine's cold q6 is 16.6 ms where the old code's was 13.9 ms, and its cold sort-topk 39.2 ms where the old code's was 37.0 ms. And the join's probe path says the opposite: the engine's own batch is now a whole row group, and that is the minimum of the curve — the query's path costs −0.159 ms against the ladder's, where the set this increment replaces had +4.273 ms. And a smaller budget is not free either: at 4 MiB the wide shape's batch would be 20,000 rows, and the sweep's lowest rung is 62,500. And the scan's curve is about to change again, because the next increment makes the reader own the Parquet decode, and the decode is what the batch's optimum turns on. What would answer it: the budget's number, measured with the decode in place — not a smaller number chosen on a guess now.
